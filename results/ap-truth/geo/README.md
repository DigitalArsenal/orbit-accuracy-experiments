# Geostationary orbits against truth

Report only, registered in `experiments/operator-theory/GEO-TRUTH.md`. Tables: `geo-truth.md`, `geo-truth.json`; burns: `burns-waas.json`, `burns-beidou.json`.

No precise orbit of an Intelsat, SES or Eutelsat satellite is published. Galaxy 30 (Intelsat), SES-15 and Eutelsat 117 West B carry WAAS payloads, and WAAS broadcasts each one's position with a stated range accuracy of 2 m (`lib/sbas.mjs`). On 2026-10-29 that broadcast and Intelsat's own Galaxy 30 file differ by a median of 1.98 km. Between burns, FFM fitted to 24 h of Galaxy 30's broadcast predicts it one day ahead to a median of 0.060 km, so the broadcast is smooth to tens of meters.

Fitted to 24 h of the broadcast, one day ahead, Galaxy 30's medians are SGP4 2.46 km, whiteroom XP 0.221 km, Space Force XP 0.535 km, AP 0.186 km and FFM 0.060 km. By day 3 every arm is near 9 km and by day 7 near 20 km, together, because Galaxy 30 burns about once a week, alternating north-south and east-west, and no arm knows about a burn. The registered burn test finds the north-south burns (each turning the plane about 12 millidegrees: 09-03, 09-17, 10-01, 10-15, 10-29). It misses the east-west ones: its threshold is 10 times the median day-to-day break over the three satellites, and SES-15's makes that 1.5 km. Galaxy 30's one-day mean semimajor axis falls about 50 m a day and steps up 300 to 400 m near 09-25 and 10-10, an east-west burn each. SES-15's swings about 400 m on a cycle of about two days, and Eutelsat 117 West B's is similar, the pattern of near-continuous station keeping; for them every arm is 1.4 to 5 km off on day 1. These rows measure the operators' burns more than the propagators.

Fitted instead to Intelsat's own Galaxy 30 file and measured against the broadcast, whiteroom XP, Space Force XP, AP and FFM are 1.0 to 1.6 km off on day 1 when no burn falls in it (6.7 to 6.8 km when the 10-28 burn does), about the file's own distance from the broadcast; SGP4 is 1.2 to 2.1 km; Intelsat's own forecast is 0.96 and 1.45 km off on day 1 of the two files the broadcast reaches so far.

BeiDou and QZSS geostationary satellites against GFZ's precise orbits (`AP6.md`): C011, C018, C229 and C231 each burn once in October 2025 inside a day the product leaves out (C011 +2.3 km in semimajor axis on 10-14, C018 -3.6 km on 10-10, C229 -0.6 km on 10-08, C231 +0.6 km on 10-03 and -1.4 km on 10-15), and QZS-2G turns its plane by 0.10 degree on 10-18. Without the windows that hold one, the day-7 medians are AP 0.150 km, whiteroom XP 0.168 km, FFM 0.264 km, Space Force XP 0.257 km and SGP4 14.8 km. That rule was written after seeing three of these satellites go wrong together, and is labeled so.
